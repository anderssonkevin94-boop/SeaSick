# SeaSick open-deck stern paddle ship — recreation brief

## Required design

- Broad wooden monohull, pointed upswept bow, rounded/faceted sides, broad squared stern with a center U-shaped paddle recess.
- Single oversized wooden paddle drum integrated into the back center. Hull cheeks support both ends of a transverse steel axle. Wheel rotates fore/aft around that axle. Keep it a defining feature.
- Fixed low wooden splash housing over the wheel's upper sector, anchored to the stern cheeks, with teal rim, two steel reinforcing straps and sparse bolts. Use a few broad curved facets. Provide clearance for the complete rotating blade envelope. Rear and lower paddle remain exposed. The roof must not rotate with the wheel.
- One continuous open main deck. No rear cabin, raised quarterdeck, cargo boxes, crates or barrels.
- Tall simple black chimney centered across the beam at midships. Steam engine and firebox below deck.
- Open steering wheel near the stern just ahead of the paddle guard, slightly to port if needed for clearance. Helmsman faces the bow.
- Six cannon mounting positions, three per broadside, barrels pointing outward across the gunwales. Keep cannons and crew modular so custom versions can be fitted.
- Clear servicing space behind each cannon and connected walking lanes across the deck. Low open rails preserve visibility of crew from rear chase camera.
- Warm flat timber colors, muted teal trim, dark steel bands, sparse large bolts. Minimal plank seams, no fine grain or decorative clutter.

## Assembly and validation

Separate hull, deck, fixed paddle hood, rotating paddle, helm, chimney, gun sockets and crew sockets. Set paddle pivot at axle center. Use project conventions for scale and axes; these drawings specify no exact dimensions.

Check the rotating wheel clears hood and hull at every angle. Judge deck/crew visibility from the existing low rear gameplay camera before adding detail. Keep the large paddle and its roof low enough that they do not become another cabin-shaped obstruction. Crew figures in the images are scale/layout references.
